# Mutatis Mutandis: Semantic and Ideological Transformation in Early Modern English Pamphlets

This project investigates semantic and ideological change in early modern English pamphlets (1600–1700), with particular focus on the period surrounding the English Civil War. It combines computational methods—diachronic distributional semantics, embedding alignment, and network analysis—with intellectual history frameworks drawn from Skinner and Koselleck to examine how key political, legal, and moral concepts such as liberty, authority, conscience, and obedience shift over time. Treating these concepts as relational “poles” within contested semantic fields, the project traces patterns of semantic drift and instability across a large EEBO-TCP corpus, identifying moments of heightened conceptual tension. These computational findings are then interpreted through close reading of selected pamphlet passages, situating semantic change within rhetorical and ideological struggle. The project aims to show how evolving patterns of language reflect broader transformations in political thought, including the gradual emergence of more secular and institutionalised forms of authority.
