### Central research question

* **What is the historical depiction of exceptional whiteness in the British Isles, and has it changed over time?**

### Core object of study

* “Exceptional whiteness” rather than whiteness in general.
* Unusually white:
  * hair;
  * skin/body;
  * clothing or bodily radiance where it is specifically associated with the figure;
  * eyes or other unusual physical features.
* Both **literal/physical whiteness** and its **symbolic representation**.
* Particular attention to figures for whom exceptional whiteness marks them as:

  * divine;
  * ancient;
  * wise;
  * pure;
  * beautiful;
  * heroic;
  * supernatural;
  * Otherworldly;
  * uncanny;
  * monstrous;
  * evil or villainous.

### Historical starting point

* **1 Enoch**
  * 1 Enoch's Noah's parents ambigious reaction
  * Important comparative evidence for the earlier Jewish/Second Temple semantic field.
  * Not necessarily an Insular influence.
  * Useful for establishing that the associations predate Christianity.
* **Daniel 7:9**
  * Ancient of Days.
  * Clothing white as snow.
  * Hair like pure wool.
  * Potential association with age, antiquity, authority, eternity and divinity.
* **Daniel 10**
  * Luminous supernatural figure.
  * White/bright imagery alongside extraordinary eyes and other bodily features.

### Christian transformation/reception

* **Revelation 1:13–16**
  * Christ's head/hair white like wool and snow.
  * Deliberate Danielic imagery.
  * White hair potentially carrying several simultaneous meanings:
    * antiquity;
    * wisdom;
    * authority;
    * eternity;
    * divinity;
    * purity;
    * radiance.

* **Patristic interpretation**
  * Origen and others.
  * Investigate what authoritative interpreters actually said rather than imposing modern symbolism.

* **Insular Christianity**
  * Bede particularly important.
  * Early glosses and commentaries potentially preserve several distinct interpretations.
  * One particularly important line: **white = aged/ancient**.
  * Other evidence apparently connects white with **divinity**.
* The question is therefore not simply *“Did Revelation borrow from Daniel?”* but
  * **How was that inherited symbolism understood in the British Isles?**
  * **Which meanings survived, changed or were added?**

### Medieval Insular material
* **Old English**
  * Anglo-Saxon biblical/exegetical material.
  * Beowulf's faer?
  * Bede apparently removing white as 'magical' symbol -> ancientness

* **Irish/Gaelic**
  * Comparative material where feasible: Finn is rich.

* **Cornish**
  * Worth checking, although evidence is sparse.

* **Old Norse/Germanic**
  * Baldr is a fine depiction - Snorri may just be reflecting Rev

* **Arthurian/Welsh material**
  * **Olwen**;
  * **Gwyn ap Nudd**;
  * *gwyn/gwen* personal names and epithets;
  * the Very White Witch;
  * the Black Witch;
  * other extraordinary white figures in *Culhwch ac Olwen*;
  * material from *Pwyll* and *Manawydan*.
  * Investigate whether *gwyn/gwen* is disproportionately associated with:
    * beauty;
    * heroism;
    * supernatural status;
    * Otherworldliness;
    * liminality.
* Do not _assume_ Christianity created this association.
* Instead investigate possible interaction between:
  * inherited Celtic semantics;
  * Christian symbolism;
  * literary convention.

* **Comparative Celtic/Germanic question**
* Compare Welsh *gwyn/gwen* with Irish *fionn/find* where useful.
* Investigate Old English and Old Norse independently.
* Ask whether recurring associations suggest:
  * common PIE inheritance;
  * cultural borrowing;
  * Christian convergence;
  * independent development;
  * or (probably) some combination.

### Albinism

* **Albinism is an explicit secondary strand, not the entire subject.**
* Do not retrospectively diagnose historical characters as albino.
* Instead record when exceptional whiteness is accompanied by other unusual physical characteristics.
* Particularly important:
  * unusual eyes;
  * unusually light eyes;
  * red/pink eyes;
  * photophobia;
  * impaired vision;
  * other physiological descriptions.

### The major diachronic question

* Did the cultural meaning of exceptional whiteness change?
* Possible trajectory to investigate:
  * divine;
  * ancient;
  * wise;
  * pure;
  * beautiful;
  * heroic;
  * supernatural / Otherworldly;
  * unusual / abnormal;
  * pathological;
  * uncanny;
  * monstrous;
  * villainous.
* Positive and negative meanings may coexist for centuries - cf 1 Enoch.
* The project should establish when particular meanings appear, disappear, overlap or reverse

### Modern reversal

* A particularly interesting later question:
  * **When does the extremely white human cease to be primarily an auspicious/supernatural figure and become an uncanny or threatening one?**

* Investigate:
  * early modern natural history (cf Thomas White's Mancs thesis);
  * classification;
  * ethnography;
  * medicalisation;
  * secularisation;
  * Gothic literature;
  * Victorian literature;
  * twentieth-century popular culture.

* Potential modern figure:
  * the extremely white/albino person as:
    * abnormal;
    * uncanny;
    * monstrous;
    * sinister (Magneto);
    * villainous (Eco).

### Key hypothesis to test

* There may be a long-term semantic movement from:

  **exceptional whiteness to exceptional status**

  towards:

  **exceptional whiteness to abnormal status**

  and eventually, in some modern genres:

  **abnormal whiteness to monstrous/evil status**.

### What the project is *not*

* Not simply a history of the colour white.
* Not simply a study of Daniel and Revelation.
* Not simply a study of Celtic colour symbolism.
* Not a history of albinism.
* Not an argument that historical white figures were secretly albino.
* Not an argument that Christianity invented positive associations with whiteness.
* Not an argument that modern negative associations necessarily descend directly from medieval Christian symbolism.
* Not a search for one single origin.

### What potentially makes it original

* The individual components have substantial scholarship behind them.
* The likely gap is the **synthetic diachronic history**:

  * Jewish/biblical semantic background;
  * Christian interpretation;
  * Insular reception;
  * Welsh/Celtic and Germanic literary traditions;
  * early modern bodily classification;
  * Gothic;
  * modern representations of extreme whiteness/albinism.
* The project asks whether these apparently separate traditions form a **historical pattern of changing representations of exceptional human whiteness**.

### The project in one sentence

> A diachronic investigation of how exceptional human whiteness has been represented and interpreted in the British Isles, from biblical and early Christian traditions through Insular and medieval literature to the modern association of extreme whiteness with abnormality, monstrosity and the albino figure.
