Why did the typology of Baldr change between the Eddic and Snorri renditions and the Saxo edition? What happened in that time?

Was it Christians removing a typological competitor to the Ancient of Days type, John of Patmos' Christ?

Was it a conscious construction of a narrative with causality, rivalry, motive and action.

The Eddic story is: cosmic tragedy caused by supernatural deception.

Saxo's story is: human rivalry complicated by supernatural forces.

If Saxo merely needed a more exciting story, he could have made Balderus a noble warrior who tragically loses Nanna.

Instead, he gives him lust, aggression and something approaching predatory behaviour.

Meanwhile, the positive qualities migrate toward Hotherus:

    Hotherus is a human → admirable hero → legitimate husband → successful king

On the whole:

    Balderus → desires Nanna → Nanna loves Hotherus → Balderus attacks → Hotherus retaliates → Balderus dies.

So:

1. Christian typological pressure - The radiant/pure/good pagan god is too close to an available Christian ideal and gets morally differentiated.
2. Euhemerising historiography - Once gods become historical actors, their supernatural perfection is redistributed into ordinary heroic virtues and vices.
3. An older Danish tradition already differed from the Icelandic one - Saxo may not be responsible for the whole inversion. He may have inherited a Balder/Hother story in which Balder was already a more aggressive, heroic figure.

### Latin

The simple fact that Saxo is in Latin suggests an interpretation boy a different culture of different facet of a greater culture...

Saxo was Danish, and his subject matter is overwhelmingly Danish/Norse, but the Gesta Danorum is written in Latin, the learned international language of medieval Europe. That means he was not simply recording a vernacular Danish version in Danish; he was recasting Scandinavian material through a Latin literary and Christian intellectual vocabulary.

Saxo's Latin gives him access to a ready-made vocabulary in which brightness/beauty and moral goodness can be closely associated, but also one in which physical beauty can be treated as an incitement to lust.

| Semantic field                     | Latin vocabulary                               | Saxo’s use with Hotherus                                            | Saxo’s use with Balderus                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Beauty**                         | *decor, pulchritudo, pulcher, venustus, forma* | Beauty is one possible basis for love                               | Beauty becomes the trigger for desire                                        |
| **Brightness / lustre**            | *nitor, candor*                                | *candor* of physical beauty; later Hotherus receives a shining belt | *nitor* describes the lustre of **Nanna’s** body, not Balderus               |
| **Virtue / excellence**            | *virtus*                                       | Central: Hotherus's youthful *virtus* attracts Nanna                | Not a defining quality                                                       |
| **Moral worth**                    | *probitas*                                     | Explicitly contrasted with mere physical appearance                 | Absent from his portrait                                                     |
| **Love**                           | *amor*                                         | Reciprocal attraction between Nanna and Hotherus                    | *infinito amore*, but immediately sexualised                                 |
| **Sexual desire**                  | *libido*                                       | Not attached to Hotherus                                            | Central: *libidinis irritamentum* — beauty as a stimulus to lust             |
| **Inflaming / passion**            | *inflammare, exardescere*                      | Nanna is attracted by Hotherus's *virtus*                           | Balderus *exarsit* after seeing Nanna bathing                                |
| **Violence**                       | *ferro tollere*                                | Hotherus ultimately fights his supernatural enemies                 | Balderus decides to kill Hotherus to remove the obstacle to his desire       |
| **Moral judgement**                | *odium*                                        | Hotherus is the hero                                                | Balderus is *infestissimo odio dignus* — “worthy of the most intense hatred” |
| **Supernatural status**            | *semideus*                                     | Hotherus remains fundamentally human                                | Balderus is explicitly called a *semideus*                                   |
| **Radiance transferred elsewhere** | *nitor*                                        | Hotherus receives a remarkable shining belt                         | No bodily radiance for Balderus                                              |
