# Nordic Albino-like Figures

## Chronology of Sources

| Source / tradition              | Date                                                   | What we actually have                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Older Eddic tradition**       | c. **Viking Age 900–1270** for most surviving material | Oral poetry and tradition; individual poems vary considerably                      |
| **Christianisation of Iceland** | **1000**                                               | Important context for the later recording and transmission of Norse tradition      |
| **Saxo Grammaticus**            | c. **1180–1208**                                       | *Gesta Danorum*, including **Balderus** and **Hotherus**                           |
| **Snorri Sturluson**            | c. **1220**                                            | *Prose Edda*, including the detailed prose account of Baldr                        |
| **Codex Regius**                | c. **1270**                                            | Main manuscript of the *Poetic Edda*, containing *Völuspá*, *Baldrs draumar*, etc. |

A fundamental chronological caution is necessary: the dates of the surviving manuscripts are not the dates at which the underlying stories were composed. The Eddic material represents traditions of varying antiquity that were transmitted orally before being committed to writing.

# Balðraz / Baldr

| **Eddic Baldr** c. 900–1270*                                 | **Snorri's Baldr** c. 1220                                                    | **Saxo's Balderus** c. 1200                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| extraordinarily bright / implied rather than fully described | **extraordinarily bright** — light shines from him                            | no comparable physical luminosity                                 |
| **beautiful / good**                                         | **fairest of feature**; his body and hair are compared with the whitest plant | beauty becomes an object of **desire**                            |
| beloved                                                      | **all praise him / best of the Æsir**                                         | **rejected by Nanna**                                             |
| —                                                            | **wisest of the Æsir**                                                        | primarily powerful and martial                                    |
| —                                                            | **fairest-spoken**                                                            | aggressive / warlike                                              |
| —                                                            | **most gracious / merciful**                                                  | sexually aroused by Nanna bathing                                 |
| —                                                            | **his judgments cannot be gainsaid**                                          | aggressively attempts to remove his rival                         |
| purity associated with him                                   | **Breiðablik: nothing unclean can enter**                                     | no corresponding purity                                           |
| innocent victim                                              | **innocent victim**                                                           | aggressor in the conflict                                         |
| killed through Loki's deception                              | killed through **Höðr + Loki's deception**                                    | killed by a **human rival**                                       |
| death destabilises the cosmos                                | death is the catastrophe leading toward **Ragnarök**                          | death becomes part of a **heroic feud**                           |
| returns after Ragnarök                                       | **returns after Ragnarök**                                                    | no equivalent restoration                                         |
| —                                                            | **son of Odin, god among the Æsir**                                           | **semi-divine son of Othinus**, but treated within heroic history |

*The Eddic column should be treated as a composite of several traditions rather than as evidence for a single fixed early “Eddic Baldr.”

The important feature of Snorri's account is not simply that Baldr is “white”. He is presented through a tightly connected semantic complex of **brightness, whiteness, beauty, purity, wisdom, eloquence, mercy and divine excellence**. His extraordinary appearance is part of a larger representation of him as an almost perfectly positive supernatural figure.

---

# Heimdallr: an independent Norse whiteness type

Baldr is not the only Æsir associated with exceptional whiteness.

*Þrymskviða* describes **Heimdallr as *hvítastr ása*** — conventionally, **“whitest of the Æsir.”** Heimdallr is also characterised by extraordinary perception and knowledge: he can see extraordinarily far, hear exceptionally well, and possesses knowledge of the future.

This creates an important parallel:

| Figure              | Exceptional whiteness / brightness                    | Exceptional perception / cognition         | Supernatural status |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Baldr**           | extraordinarily bright; exceptionally white/beautiful | wisest / judgments authoritative           | god                 |
| **Heimdallr**       | **whitest of the Æsir**                               | extraordinary sight, hearing and foresight | god                 |
| **Saxo's Balderus** | no equivalent bodily radiance                         | no corresponding wisdom                    | semi-divine         |
| **Saxo's Hotherus** | receives remarkable shining equipment                 | cultivated human abilities / *virtus*      | human               |

Heimdallr is particularly interesting for the wider project because the combination is not simply **white = beautiful**. Here exceptional whiteness is attached to a being distinguished by **extraordinary sensory perception and knowledge**.

However, the evidence should not be pushed beyond what the texts establish. *Hvítastr* clearly establishes an exceptional whiteness, but it does not by itself establish **albinism**, nor does it necessarily describe a medical phenotype. The importance is the existence of an early Norse literary association between **exceptional whiteness and exceptional being**.

---

# Baldr and the Positive Typology

The positive Baldr type can therefore be provisionally described as:

**exceptional whiteness / brightness**
↓
**exceptional beauty**
↓
**purity**
↓
**wisdom / authoritative judgement**
↓
**graciousness / mercy**
↓
**divine excellence**

This is unusually close to the broader problem being investigated in the project: how exceptional whiteness can become a marker not merely of colour, but of **alterity, purity, authority, antiquity, supernatural status or exceptional perception**.

That does not establish a connection between Baldr and the biblical “white-as-wool” figures. Rather, it supplies comparative evidence that northern European traditions could construct an exceptional white figure through a semantic package extending well beyond ordinary colour description.

# Saxo: the Inversion of Balderus

Saxo's account is striking because the positive qualities associated with the Icelandic Baldr are not simply removed. They are **redistributed**.

### Positive type

| Hotherus                                | Balderus                               |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| *virtus* / courage                      | *libido* / lust                        |
| cultivated accomplishments              | overwhelming physical power            |
| wins Nanna's affection                  | seeks to possess Nanna                 |
| courtesy / social virtue                | violence against rival                 |
| human                                   | supernatural                           |
| defeats supernatural enemies            | divine status without moral legitimacy |
| receives shining supernatural equipment | loses obvious radiant-body symbolism   |

The resulting narrative is fundamentally different.

### Eddic / Snorri

**cosmic tragedy caused by supernatural deception**

Baldr is an innocent and beloved divine figure whose death is a catastrophe for the cosmos.

### Saxo

**human rivalry complicated by supernatural forces**

Balderus desires Nanna → Nanna loves Hotherus → Balderus attacks → Hotherus retaliates → Balderus dies.

This is not merely a change in plot mechanics. It is a transformation in **moral typology**.

If Saxo merely needed a more exciting story, he could have made Balderus a noble supernatural warrior who tragically loses Nanna. Instead, he gives him **lust, aggression and something approaching predatory behaviour**, while transferring the positive qualities of the heroic type to Hotherus.

The question therefore becomes:

> **Why are the characteristics that make Baldr an exceptionally bright, beautiful and morally exemplary supernatural figure redistributed between Balderus and Hotherus in Saxo, with Balderus receiving precisely those characteristics — lust and aggression — that disqualify him as an exemplary figure?**

---

# Latin and Christian Historiographical Context

Saxo was Danish and his subject matter is overwhelmingly Scandinavian, but the *Gesta Danorum* was written in **Latin**, the learned international language of medieval Christian Europe.

He was therefore not simply recording a vernacular Danish version of the story. He was **recasting Scandinavian material through a Latin literary and Christian intellectual vocabulary**.

This matters because Latin provides a vocabulary in which several semantic relationships relevant to the transformation are readily expressible:

| Semantic field                     | Latin vocabulary                               | Hotherus                                                      | Balderus                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Beauty**                         | *decor, pulchritudo, pulcher, venustus, forma* | Beauty can contribute to love                                 | Beauty becomes the trigger for desire                                      |
| **Brightness / lustre**            | *nitor, candor*                                | *candor* of physical beauty; later receives shining equipment | *nitor* describes the lustre of **Nanna's** body rather than Balderus      |
| **Virtue / excellence**            | *virtus*                                       | Central: youthful *virtus* attracts Nanna                     | Not a defining quality                                                     |
| **Moral worth**                    | *probitas*                                     | Explicitly distinguished from mere appearance                 | Absent from his portrait                                                   |
| **Love**                           | *amor*                                         | Reciprocal attraction between Nanna and Hotherus              | *infinito amore*, immediately sexualised                                   |
| **Sexual desire**                  | *libido*                                       | Not attached to Hotherus                                      | Central: *libidinis irritamentum* — beauty as a stimulus to lust           |
| **Inflaming / passion**            | *inflammare, exardescere*                      | Nanna is attracted by Hotherus's *virtus*                     | Balderus *exarsit* after seeing Nanna bathing                              |
| **Violence**                       | *ferro tollere*                                | Hotherus ultimately fights his supernatural enemies           | Balderus decides to kill Hotherus to remove the obstacle to his desire     |
| **Moral judgement**                | *odium*                                        | Hotherus is the hero                                          | Balderus is *infestissimo odio dignus* — worthy of the most intense hatred |
| **Supernatural status**            | *semideus*                                     | Hotherus remains fundamentally human                          | Balderus is explicitly a *semideus*                                        |
| **Radiance transferred elsewhere** | *nitor* / shining imagery                      | Hotherus receives a remarkable shining belt                   | No bodily radiance for Balderus                                            |

The Latin therefore gives Saxo a particularly useful vocabulary for separating **physical beauty from moral virtue** and **beauty from sexual desire**.

# Three Possible Explanations

At present, three explanations should be treated as competing or complementary hypotheses.

## 1. Christian typological pressure

A pagan figure who is simultaneously **radiant, pure, beautiful, good, wise and beloved** risks resembling an available Christian ideal.

The Christian literary environment therefore provides a plausible reason for differentiating Balderus morally from an idealised sacred figure.

The hypothesis is **not** that Saxo consciously set out to “destroy” a pagan Christ substitute. That would require evidence we do not currently have.

The more defensible proposition is:

> Christian intellectual culture may have created a pressure against allowing a pagan supernatural figure to retain an uncomplicated package of radiance, purity, beauty and moral perfection.

This is particularly worth testing against Saxo's treatment of other pagan figures.

---

## 2. Euhemerising historiography

Saxo repeatedly converts supernatural mythology into something resembling human history.

Once gods become historical actors, the characteristics appropriate to divine myth need to be redistributed into:

* human virtues;
* human vices;
* political motives;
* sexual motives;
* military conflicts;
* dynastic relationships;
* individual actions and consequences.

The transformation therefore becomes:

**divine tragedy → historical narrative**

and:

**cosmic opposition → human rivalry**

This explains why Saxo's version has much more explicit **causality, motive and action**.

---

## 3. An older Danish tradition already differed

Saxo may not himself have invented the entire inversion.

He could have inherited a Danish tradition in which Balder was already a more aggressive and heroic figure than the Baldr preserved in Icelandic sources.

This possibility is especially important because Saxo's work predates the surviving *Codex Regius* manuscript and is not simply derived from Snorri.

The comparison therefore cannot safely be reduced to:

**“Snorri's Baldr → Saxo's corrupted Balderus.”**

It may instead represent:

**different regional traditions → different medieval literary reconstructions.**

---

# The Central Observation

The most interesting phenomenon is therefore not simply that **Saxo's Balderus is different**.

It is that the **positive typological package appears to be redistributed**.

### Icelandic / Eddic configuration

**Baldr**

> brightness → whiteness → beauty → purity → wisdom → mercy → divine excellence

### Saxo's configuration

**Balderus**

> supernatural status → physical power → beauty → desire → lust → aggression → moral condemnation

**Hotherus**

> humanity → *virtus* → cultivated ability → reciprocal love → legitimate relationship → heroic victory → kingship

And even the **radiance** appears to migrate: Balderus loses the obvious radiant-body symbolism while Hotherus subsequently receives remarkable shining supernatural equipment.

This makes the Saxo account potentially valuable evidence for a larger question:

> **How does an exceptional-whiteness or exceptional-radiance type change when a supernatural figure is transformed from a mythological being into a historical/heroic actor?**

That question is more securely supported by the evidence than the stronger claim that Saxo consciously remodelled Balderus in opposition to Christ.

---

# Relevance to the Wider Study

This Nordic material should therefore be treated as **comparative evidence**, rather than as evidence for a direct transmission line from biblical descriptions of Christ to Baldr.

The wider research question is:

> **What is the historical depiction of exceptional whiteness in the British Isles, and has it changed over time?**

The particular interest here is in cases where exceptional whiteness is associated with other unusual characteristics — especially **unusual eyes, exceptional perception, beauty, antiquity, purity, supernatural status or marginality**.

Baldr and Heimdallr provide useful comparative cases because they demonstrate that northern European literary traditions could use exceptional whiteness as part of a wider construction of an **exceptional person/being**, without that necessarily meaning ordinary pigmentation.

The Saxo/Snorri comparison then supplies something more unusual: a case in which the **same broad mythological complex appears to undergo a transformation in which exceptional appearance and supernatural status survive, but moral value and agency are radically rearranged**.

This makes the Baldr/Balderus material relevant not because Baldr was necessarily an “albino” in any modern medical sense, but because it allows investigation of the historical semantics of **exceptional whiteness as a marker of difference**.
