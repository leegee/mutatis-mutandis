| Date / Stage                                      | Event / Description                                                                 | Notes / Correction |
|---------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| **1. Ordinary World**                             | Personal rule of Charles I                                                          | 1629–1640. Proclamations & sermons. “Eleven Years’ Tyranny” is a later hostile label; historians prefer **Personal Rule**. Ship money extended inland in 1635. |
| **1625**                                          | Charles I becomes king; early tensions begin                                        | Correct. Succeeded March 1625. Conflict arose over taxation, forced loans, religion, and failed wars with Spain and France. |
| **1628**                                          | Petition of Right                                                                   | Accepted by Charles I in **June 1628**. Protested taxation without consent, arbitrary imprisonment, martial law, and forced billeting. |
| **1629–1640**                                     | Personal Rule                                                                       | See Ordinary World above. |
| **1637–1640**                                     | Scottish Prayer Book crisis → Bishops’ Wars                                         | Riots 1637; National Covenant **1638**; Bishops’ Wars **1639–1640**. |
| **2. Call to Adventure**                          | Breakdown of royal authority                                                        | 1640–1642. Petitions & pamphlets. |
| **April 1640**                                    | Short Parliament                                                                    | Sat **13 April–5 May 1640** (three weeks). John Pym (MP for Tavistock) emerged as a leading critic. |
| **November 1640**                                 | Long Parliament opens                                                               | Strafford impeached 1640, executed May 1641; Laud impeached 1640, executed 1645. |
| **October 1641**                                  | Irish Rebellion                                                                     | Began October 1641. “Massacres of Protestants” reflects contemporary English fears and propaganda; scale and nature remain debated. |
| **3. Refusal of the Call**                        | Attempts to preserve monarchy                                                       | 1641–1642. Royal declarations. |
| **1641–1642**                                     | Triennial Act, militia crisis, Five Members                                         | **Triennial Act** passed February 1641. Militia crisis and attempted arrest of the Five Members on **4 January 1642**. |
| **22 August 1642**                                | Charles raises his standard at Nottingham                                           | Conventional start of the First Civil War. |
| **4. Meeting the Mentor**                         | Parliamentary ideology forms                                                        | 1642–1644. Newsbooks. |
| **23 October 1642**                               | Battle of Edgehill                                                                  | First major pitched battle; strategically inconclusive. |
| **20 September 1643**                             | First Battle of Newbury                                                             | Parliamentarian victory that helped prevent Charles taking London. |
| **September 1643**                                | Solemn League and Covenant                                                          | Signed by English Parliamentarians and Scots; Scottish military intervention followed. |
| **5. Crossing the Threshold**                     | Civil war becomes total                                                             | 1643–1645. Ordinances. |
| **2 July 1644**                                   | Battle of Marston Moor                                                              | Largest battle of the wars; Parliamentarian/Scottish victory secured northern England. |
| **1644–1645**                                     | Self-Denying Ordinance & New Model Army                                             | New Model Army created **February 1645**; Self-Denying Ordinance passed **April 1645**. Fairfax commander-in-chief; Cromwell lieutenant-general of the horse. |
| **6. Tests, Allies, Enemies**                     | Factionalisation & New Model Army                                                   | 1644–1647. Military tracts. |
| **14 June 1645**                                  | Battle of Naseby                                                                    | Decisive Parliamentarian victory; destroyed Charles I’s main field army. |
| **5 May 1646**                                    | Charles surrenders to the Scots near Newark                                         | Correct. |
| **1647**                                          | Charles on the Isle of Wight                                                        | Charles **escaped Hampton Court** (Nov 1647) and went to the Isle of Wight, where he was confined (mainly at Carisbrooke). He negotiated the **Engagement** with the Scots (Dec 1647). He did not escape *to* freedom there. |
| **7. Approach to the Inmost Cave**                | Trial of sovereignty itself                                                         | 1648. Army remonstrances. |
| **1648**                                          | Second Civil War                                                                    | Scottish invasion under the Duke of Hamilton in support of Charles I. |
| **17–19 August 1648**                             | Battle of Preston                                                                   | Cromwell defeated the Scottish Royalist army. |
| **6 December 1648**                               | Pride’s Purge                                                                       | Around **100–150 MPs** excluded or prevented from sitting (“140” is a common estimate; totals vary by source). |
| **8. Ordeal**                                     | Regicide                                                                            | 1649. Act establishing the High Court of Justice. |
| **January 1649**                                  | Trial and execution of Charles I                                                    | Trial began 20 January; execution 30 January 1649. |
| **9. Reward**                                     | Commonwealth proclaimed                                                             | 1649–1653. Acts & proclamations. |
| **1649–1650**                                     | Cromwell in Ireland                                                                 | Drogheda and Wexford 1649; Cromwell left Ireland May 1650. |
| **1650–1651**                                     | Third Civil War                                                                     | Charles II crowned in Scotland January 1651 before invading England. |
| **1653**                                          | Barebones Parliament / Protectorate                                                 | Nominated Assembly dissolved December 1653. Cromwell became Lord Protector under the Instrument of Government (Dec 1653). |
| **1657**                                          | Humble Petition and Advice                                                          | Cromwell refused the crown but accepted revised constitutional arrangements. |
| **3 September 1658**                              | Death of Oliver Cromwell                                                            | Richard Cromwell succeeded but resigned 1659. |
| **1659–1660**                                     | Political instability                                                               | Army, Rump Parliament, and factions struggled for control. |
| **10. The Road Back**                             | Restoration of monarchy                                                             | 1660. Declaration of Breda. |
| **May 1660**                                      | Restoration of Charles II                                                           | Charles II returned after the Declaration of Breda (April 1660). |
| **1661–1679**                                     | Cavalier Parliament                                                                 | Sat 1661–1679; passed the Clarendon Code (1661–1665). |
| **1665**                                          | Great Plague + Second Anglo-Dutch War                                               | War began 1665; plague peaked 1665–1666. |
| **1666**                                          | Great Fire of London                                                                | Correct. (Medway Raid was **June 1667**, not 1666.) |
| **1667**                                          | Raid on the Medway                                                                  | Dutch naval victory; severe blow to English prestige. |
| **1670**                                          | Secret Treaty of Dover                                                              | Signed secretly; contributed to the Third Anglo-Dutch War. |
| **1672–1674**                                     | Third Anglo-Dutch War                                                               | England withdrew after financial and political difficulties. |
| **1673**                                          | Test Act                                                                            | Required officeholders to take Anglican communion and reject Catholic doctrine. |
| **1678**                                          | Popish Plot                                                                         | Fabricated conspiracy promoted by Titus Oates; intense anti-Catholic reaction. |
| **1679–1681**                                     | Exclusion Crisis                                                                    | Parliament attempted to exclude James, Duke of York, from the succession. |
| **1683**                                          | Rye House Plot                                                                      | Alleged plot against Charles II and James; led to executions of some opposition figures. |
| **February 1685**                                 | Death of Charles II; accession of James II                                          | Correct. |
| **11. Resurrection**                              | Crisis of absolutism returns                                                        | 1685–1688. Royal dispensations. |
| **June–July 1685**                                | Monmouth Rebellion                                                                  | Began **June 1685**; Battle of Sedgemoor fought **6 July 1685**. |
| **1687**                                          | Declaration of Indulgence                                                           | James II suspended penal laws against Catholics and Protestant dissenters. |
| **June 1688**                                     | Birth of James Francis Edward Stuart                                                | “Warming-pan” substitution story is contemporary propaganda; rejected by historians. |
| **November–December 1688**                        | Glorious Revolution                                                                 | William of Orange landed at Torbay on **5 November 1688**; James II fled in December. |
| **12. Return with the Elixir**                    | Constitutional settlement                                                           | 1689. Bill of Rights. |
| **1689**                                          | Bill of Rights & accession of William and Mary                                      | William and Mary accepted the crown in February 1689; Bill of Rights enacted December 1689. |
| **1689**                                          | Toleration Act                                                                      | Limited toleration for Protestant nonconformists (not Catholics or atheists). |
